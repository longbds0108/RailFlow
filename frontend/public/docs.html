RailFlow protocol01

What is RailFlow?

RailFlow is a stablecoin app on Arc Testnet that does five things: send, swap, stake, bridge — and schedule any of those to run later. You keep your keys the whole time.

Think of it as a payments account you actually own. A bank moves your money for you, on its schedule, with its permission. RailFlow prepares the transaction and hands it to your wallet — nothing moves until you sign it in MetaMask. The app never holds your funds and has no way to move them.

What you can do

Send

Move USDC, EURC or cirBTC to any wallet address on Arc Testnet.

Swap

Trade between USDC, EURC and cirBTC on Arc. Priced by the pool, not an order book.

Stake

Put idle USDC or EURC to work and earn USDC rewards at a fixed demo rate.

Bridge

Move USDC between Arc, Ethereum Sepolia and Base Sepolia over Circle's CCTP. Always native USDC — never wrapped.

Jobs

Set an action once — "500 USDC on the 1st of every month" — and RailFlow prepares each run for you to sign.

Assistant

Describe what you want in plain language. Get back a transaction plan you can read before you approve it.

Supported assets

USDC EURC cirBTC

Networks

Arc Testnet Ethereum Sepolia Base Sepolia

Arc Testnet is home. Sepolia and Base Sepolia are bridge endpoints — you can move USDC to and from them, but swapping, staking and jobs all run on Arc.

Testnet only

Every asset here is a test asset with no market value. The staking rate is a fixed demo number, not real yield. Don't send anything you'd miss.

Getting started02

Getting started

Four steps, about five minutes. You need MetaMask and nothing else.

Add Arc Testnet to MetaMask

Open testnet.arcscan.app and use the Add Arc Testnet control at the bottom of the page, then confirm in the wallet popup. Network details are also on docs.arc.network if you'd rather add it by hand.

Get testnet tokens

Go to faucet.circle.com, select Arc Testnet, and request tokens. You'll receive both USDC and EURC. Gas on Arc is paid in USDC, so the same balance covers your fees.

Connect your wallet

Open RailFlow and connect. There's no account to create and no email to hand over — your address is your account. If you're on the wrong network, the app will offer to switch you.

Send a small amount first

Send 1 USDC to a second address you control and confirm it arrives. That one round trip tells you the wallet, the network and the app are all talking to each other before you try anything larger.

Gas on Arc

Fees are denominated in USDC. You don't need to hold a separate gas token, and the cost of a transfer is quoted in the same unit as the transfer itself.

Core actions03

Send

Transfer USDC, EURC or cirBTC to any address on Arc Testnet.

Pick the asset, enter an amount and a recipient address, and review. RailFlow shows you the amount, the recipient and the estimated fee in USDC before anything reaches your wallet. Signing in MetaMask is the point of no return — everything before it is editable.

What to check before signing

  • The address. Transfers are final. There's no recall and no support desk that can reverse one.
  • The asset. USDC and EURC look similar in a list and are not interchangeable.
  • The network. Send works on Arc Testnet only. To move USDC to another chain, use Bridge instead.
Recurring transfers

If you're going to send the same amount to the same address regularly, set it up once under Jobs rather than repeating this flow every month.

Core actions04

Swap

Trade between USDC, EURC and cirBTC on Arc, in one transaction.

Swaps are same-chain: both sides of the trade stay on Arc Testnet. Choose what you're paying with, what you want, and the amount. RailFlow quotes the output before you commit. Prices come from pool liquidity, so larger trades move the price against you — the quote reflects that.

Under the hood

Swaps route through Circle App Kit, which abstracts the underlying protocol behind one call:

// Swap 1.00 USDC for EURC on Arc Testnet
await kit.swap({
  from:     { adapter: viemAdapter, chain: "Arc_Testnet" },
  tokenIn:  "USDC",
  tokenOut: "EURC",
  amountIn: "1.00",
});

USDC and EURC are both stablecoins but track different currencies, so a USDC ↔ EURC swap is an FX trade and the rate moves with the euro. cirBTC is volatile — quote and execute close together.

Core actions05

Stake

Deposit USDC or EURC and earn USDC rewards at a fixed demo rate.

Staking is for balances you aren't actively moving. Deposit, and rewards accrue in USDC while the position is open. Unstake whenever you want — there's no lockup on testnet.

FieldDetail

This is not yield

The APY is a constant written into the demo. There is no lending market, no borrower and no revenue behind it. It exists to make the staking flow testable — treat the number as a placeholder, not a return.

Core actions06

Bridge

Move USDC between Arc Testnet, Ethereum Sepolia and Base Sepolia using Circle's CCTP.

CCTP is burn-and-mint: your USDC is destroyed on the source chain, Circle attests to the burn, and an equal amount is minted on the destination. You always end up holding native USDC — never a wrapped IOU issued by a third-party bridge.

What happens, in order

Burn

You sign a transaction on the source chain. Your USDC is burned. The transfer shows as pending.

Attest

Circle observes the burn and issues an attestation. This is the waiting phase — it depends on source-chain finality, not on RailFlow.

Mint

The attestation is redeemed on the destination chain and native USDC appears in your address there. The transfer is complete.

// Bridge 1.00 USDC from Ethereum Sepolia to Arc Testnet
await kit.bridge({
  from:   { adapter: viemAdapter, chain: "Ethereum_Sepolia" },
  to:     { adapter: viemAdapter, chain: "Arc_Testnet" },
  amount: "1.00",
});
If a bridge sits pending

It's almost always the attestation step. The funds are burned and accounted for — they are not lost. Check the transaction on ArcScan rather than submitting the transfer again, which would move a second amount.

USDC only. CCTP doesn't carry EURC or cirBTC. To move those, swap to USDC on Arc first, bridge, then swap back on the far side.

Automation07

Jobs

Set an action up once and let it run on a schedule — without handing over your keys.

A wallet is stateless: it does exactly what you tell it, exactly when you tell it. That's fine for a one-off transfer and painful for anything recurring — payroll, a monthly invoice, a standing transfer to a savings address. Jobs adds the missing piece.

Define the action once: asset, amount, recipient, and when it should happen. RailFlow tracks the schedule and prepares each run. Common shapes:

  • Recurring send — 500 USDC to the same address on the 1st of each month.
  • Recurring swap — convert a fixed slice of USDC to EURC every week.
  • Balance-triggered bridge — move USDC to Base when the Arc balance passes a threshold you set.

The custody trade-off, stated plainly

Every service that runs stablecoin payments on a schedule does it by taking custody — holding your keys, or your float, or both. RailFlow doesn't. The consequence is honest and worth understanding before you rely on it:

Jobs prepare. You execute.

A job cannot sign for you. When a run comes due, RailFlow builds the transaction and asks for your signature. If you don't sign, nothing moves. This is a real limitation, and it is the direct cost of the app never being able to move your funds on its own.

Unattended execution is on the roadmap via session keys with hard spend caps, recipient allowlists and an expiry — narrow, revocable authority instead of full custody. It isn't shipped yet.

Automation08

Assistant

Say what you want. Read the plan. Sign it, or don't.

Most of the friction in a stablecoin app is translation — you know you want to pay someone in euros from a dollar balance, and the app makes you turn that into a chain, two token selectors and three screens. The Assistant takes the sentence instead.

Type something like send 250 EURC to 0xa1b2… on Friday or move half my Arc USDC to Base. The Assistant resolves it into a concrete plan and shows it to you as explicit parameters: asset, amount, recipient, chain, fee.

What it cannot do

This is the part worth being precise about, because "AI that touches your money" deserves a clear boundary.

Proposes only

The Assistant builds transaction plans. It cannot sign, cannot broadcast, and holds no key material. Every plan it produces goes to your wallet for approval like any other action in RailFlow. It is an input method, not an authority.

  • Plans are always rendered as reviewable parameters — never executed from a summary.
  • If a request is ambiguous, the Assistant asks rather than guessing at an amount or an address.
  • Anything it proposes, you could have built by hand in the Send, Swap, Stake or Bridge screens. It's a shortcut, not a separate set of powers.
Reference09

Security

RailFlow is a client-side interface. It cannot move your funds, and it isn't built so that it could.

The guarantees

  • No custody. The app holds no private keys, no deposits and no float. Your assets stay at your own address.
  • No admin key. There is no privileged role that can move, freeze or claw back a user balance.
  • You sign everything. Every state change produces a MetaMask prompt. There are no background transactions.
  • The Assistant can't execute. It constructs plans; only your signature broadcasts one.
  • Jobs can't execute unsupervised. A scheduled run is prepared for you, not performed without you.

The limits, stated honestly

  • Testnet only. No mainnet deployment.
  • Not audited. Treat it as a demo, because that's what it is.
  • The staking APY is a fixed constant with no yield source behind it.
  • Bridging is USDC-only — CCTP doesn't carry EURC or cirBTC.
  • Jobs require you to be present to sign each run.
